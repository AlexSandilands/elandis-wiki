**Caer Nystral**, named **The Dawnless City**, is the capital of the [[Vale of Eternal Night]] and the seat of the Unseelie Court in the [[Faewild]]. Where [[Sumara, The Shining City|Sumara]] grows upward into prismatic light and is named the Shining City, Caer Nystral hunkers in black stone and grown blackthorn — lit only by the cold glow of its anchored dark moon, by the witch-light of its streets, and by the phosphor-fungi trailing its stairs — and is named the Dawnless, the city on which the sun has never risen and never will. The mirroring is deliberate, and pointed. It is ruled from its central height by the archfae [[Mirileth|the Star-Veiled Queen]].

## Geography & Layout

Caer Nystral sits at the heart of the [[Vale of Eternal Night]], filling an immense bowl-shaped crater in the floor of the Vale. The crater is no accident of terrain: according to the archives of the [[Circle of the Scroll]], this is the exact place where the sky fell when [[Ryordan Silmara]] sundered the World Tree — the Sundering that extinguished the light, plunged the Vale into the endless night it has never left, and gouged the bowl the city fills. The land's recoil left a central upthrust at the crater's heart, and it is on that height that the Veiled Citadel, the throne of the Star-Veiled Queen, stands.

The approach is the city's first defence. The crater floor is given over to the same hazards that make the wider Vale lethal — tideless mires that never ebb, witch-light bogs whose pale lures lead nowhere safe, and the Webs of fae spiders far older and larger than anything on the Material Plane. Only three guarded causeways cross this killing-ground to the city's gates. From the outer edge inward, the city climbs through a series of concentric rings, each walled from the last and each a little higher than the one before, until the ground crests at the Veiled Citadel in the centre. A single ceremonial avenue, the Midnight Procession, runs from the Antler Gates straight up through every ring to the Citadel at the summit.

## The Rings of the City

Caer Nystral ascends to its throne as [[Sumara, The Shining City|Sumara]] does — but in shadow, and inward. From the watched threshold to the height beneath the dark moon:

- **The Barrow-Ring** — Beyond the main wall, the outermost ground belongs to the night hag covens, who dwell in mounds and barrows ringing the city. The oldest, coldest powers of the Court are kept here at the threshold rather than within it. The Hunt marches its condemned out to this ring, and few who are marched there return — a march that, to the prisoner, can be made to look like an escort toward the Court until it bends away toward the mounds.
- **The Thornwall & the Gates** — The main outer wall is a living rampart of grown blackthorn, impassably dense and quick to grow back faster than it can be cut. It is pierced by antler-arched gates of black iron — the Antler Gates to the south, where embassies are met, and the lesser north-western and north-eastern gates — each held by sentries of the Hunt.
- **The Lower Warren** — Inside the main wall lies the teeming, lightless commons of the Quiet Folk — redcaps, quicklings, boggarts, and blights — knotted around the Nightmarket. It is not a market to browse but a warren to survive: crowded, dark, and quick to turn on the unfamiliar. It is also the one place in the city where a stranger might half-blend.
- **The Muster Yards** — Within the inner wall sits the Court's martial heart, the ground of the Hunt. Here are the worg-kennels of the Hunt's trackers and the nightmare-stables of the Fae Knights, flanking the muster field where the riding-bands form up before they ride out across the Vale. To reach the Citadel uninvited is to cross the Hunt's own ground.
- **The Court Tiers** — Higher still, cold and beautiful halls house the lesser archfae of the Court, each with a small retinue, each keeping its own counsel at the Queen's sufferance.
- **The Veiled Citadel** — At the crater's central height stands the throne of the Star-Veiled Queen, a single mass of black obsidian; from its peak rises the lunar spire that anchors the dark moon over the Vale.

## Architecture

Caer Nystral changes character as it climbs, so that a visitor can read their depth into the city by the materials around them. The outer Lower Warren is a close, organic sprawl of dark Vale timber — blackwood and silverleaf felled from the surrounding forests — roofed in grey slate and crowded so tightly that blackthorn, phosphor-fungi, and pale creeping flora knot through every gap between the walls. It is the nearest the city comes to looking lived-in: crooked, low, and green at the edges.

Inward and upward, the wood gives way to harder things. The Muster Yards and the Court Tiers are raised in black steel, dark stone, and black marble, and with each tier the design grows colder and more deliberate — straighter walls, sharper angles, roofs and spires drawn up into fae-wrong points like a thicket of antlers turned to architecture. The flora thins; the slate becomes steel; the silhouette becomes a blade.

At the summit, the Veiled Citadel is the end of that progression: a single mass of pure black obsidian, sheer and glass-dark, its antler-spires catching the pale light of the lunar spire along their edges. Where the Warren is grown, the Citadel is cut — and the whole city between them is the slow turning of one into the other.

## The Dark Moon

From the peak of the Veiled Citadel rises a lunar spire — a beam of pale light that climbs into the sky and anchors there a dark moon: a black orb that glows with the cold white light of an eclipse. It hangs fixed above the city, never crossing the sky as a true moon would, yet it still turns through the familiar phases — from a full orb glowing on every side to a thin crescent lit along one edge alone. Visible from most of the Vale, the dark moon and its spire are the surest sign of Caer Nystral seen from afar.

The dark moon is the Star-Veiled Queen's most public work. She anchored it not long after she took the throne, in the hope that a single shared light, hung where every corner of the Vale could see it, might draw her scattered and quarrelsome subjects together into one people.

## The Dwellers

No sun has touched Caer Nystral, and over uncounted generations its people have come to share its dark. Almost every native sees in the blackness without difficulty; an intruder who cannot is blind, and lit up to fae eyes like a struck match. The city is no single people but a layered arrangement — what dwells in the Barrow-Ring does not come to the Warren uninvited, and the Court Tiers maintain a deliberate distance from the press of the commons below. There are no humans here, and no warmth. At rest the city holds some twelve thousand long-lived fae; when the Court is in full residence that number climbs toward twenty-five or thirty thousand.

### The Barrow-Ring

The oldest and coldest powers of the Court are kept at its threshold rather than within it.

- Night hag covens — the most powerful presences in the ring, dwelling in earth-mounds and barrow-tombs they occupied before the city existed. Three great covens divide the ring's territory, each led by a Grand Crone whose true name is a guarded secret. They deal in dream-currency, soul-debt, and the condemned prisoners the Hunt marches out to them; they answer to the Queen in matters of bare survival and to no one else. The Grand Crone of the southernmost coven, a vast and ancient thing who calls herself [[Ymrageth]], is the ring's unofficial voice.
- Annis hags — cruder counsel than the night hags: large, iron-clawed, and contemptuous of subtlety. A scattering hold their own mounds in the ring, tolerated by the covens as useful muscle.
- Bog wraiths — pale, half-visible things that drift the mires between the barrows, tolerated by the hags as a perimeter alarm.
- Barrow-spiders — immense fae arachnids whose silk lines every mound and marsh-tree of the ring; their larger kin hold the Webs of the crater floor outside the walls.

### The Lower Warren

The city's teeming, lightless commons — crowded, dark, and fast to turn on the unfamiliar.

- Redcaps — the largest bloc in the Warren, heavy-set and quick to violence, with iron-shod boots and caps dyed red by means no sensible visitor enquires about. They fill the Nightmarket's stalls and alleys, doing most of its buying and a great deal of its settling of accounts.
- Spriggans — stunted and grotesque at rest, capable of swelling to near-giant size when provoked. They cluster at the Warren's choke-points doing extortion work the redcaps prefer not to be seen doing directly.
- Quicklings — reed-thin and almost invisible at speed. They serve as message-runners, pickpockets, scouts, and occasionally assassins; most of the Warren's rumour flows through them whether their employers intend it to or not.
- Boggarts — squat, mutable fae that shift their shape to unsettle, never quite settling on a form. They run irregular trade in the lower alleys that doesn't benefit from a steady appearance.
- Darklings — fae fully swallowed by shadow, moving through the darkest alleys as little more than shapes. They are trusted with errands that must be run in absolute secrecy; nearly invisible in full darkness, frantic and unreliable in light.
- Twig and needle blights — the Warren's porters and groundskeepers, too simple for politics, useful mainly because they work in absolute darkness and never complain.

### The Muster Yards

The Hunt's ground is not a district so much as an institution with walls.

- Fae centaurs — the Hunt's heavy cavalry, larger and wilder than the centaurs of the Material Plane, with coats in grey, midnight-blue, and deep brown. They carry the pride of warriors who have rarely lost; the Hunt's history is one of unbroken pursuit across the Vale.
- Fae knights — elf-blooded or purely fae warriors sworn to the Hunt, armoured in black steel and riding nightmare-mounts: horse-shaped fae whose smoke-manes and ember-eyes mark them as something other than horse. They observe a strict internal hierarchy and a cold formality that reads, to outsiders, as contempt.
- Worg-handlers — the kennelmasters and trackers, bonded to the giant worg packs that serve as the Hunt's nose; a rougher, more practically-minded sort than the knights.
- Yeth hounds — where the worgs run a physical trail, the yeth hounds follow something else: a supernatural scent of fear and flight that crosses no boundary a mortal can block. They are the Hunt's instrument for quarry that has gone to ground behind wards or walls.
- Night elf soldiers — the rank-and-file garrison of the gates and walls, neither knights nor riders, the steady watch on the causeways and the city's outer face.

### The Court Tiers

Cold halls, careful distance, and the intricate arithmetic of favour.

- Fae night elves — the dominant people of the Court Tiers, cold-skinned and dark-eyed, folded far more deeply into fae nature than their Sumaran kin. They are slow to laugh and long in memory, and serve as advisors, chamberlains, heralds, and minor lords in the lesser archfae retinues.
- Lesser archfae — half a dozen minor lords of the Unseelie Court occupy the Tiers in halls of their own, each with a small retinue, each keeping their own counsel at the Queen's sufferance. The competition between them is continuous but never openly violent, which the Queen does not permit.
- Court retainers and attendants — night elves, quicklings, and the occasional boggart staffing the archfae halls, who know more of Court business than anyone above them realises.

### The Veiled Citadel

The fewest in number and the highest in authority.

- Cold knights of the Hunt — the Queen's personal guard, a step above the order below: longer-serving, colder, armoured in black steel with the lunar spire's pale light hammered into the surface. Some have served long enough that the person beneath the helmet has become difficult to reach.
- The inner court — [[Mirileth]] holds a small formal court at the Citadel's peak: a handful of favoured archfae, her seneschal, and her most trusted advisors. Admission is by the Queen's invitation alone, and invitations are not returned.

## Defences

Caer Nystral is held less by formal garrison than by the nature of the place itself.

- The Hunt — the Court's standing strength of cavalry, trackers, and knights, kennelled and stabled in the Muster Yards.
- The Causeways — only three crossings span the mires, the bogs, and the Webs of the crater floor, and every one is watched.
- The Thornwall — a rampart that regrows faster than any siege can clear it.
- The Queen's eye — little crosses the Vale unseen by the Star-Veiled Queen's magic; she permits no loose ends.
- The dark itself — the oldest defence, and the one no stranger can answer.

## [[The Bloody Nails|Campaign: The Bloody Nails]]

#### [[Session 50 - A New Morning in Sumara]]

[[Warden Sylvaris]] named Caer Nystral as the party's destination: the Dawnless City, mirror of [[Sumara, The Shining City|Sumara]], seat of [[Mirileth|the Star-Veiled Queen]], and the place on whose borders [[Asha Vayne]], son of [[Kaelen Vayne]], had vanished sixty years past — alive, the Warden suspected, in the Queen's keeping. The artifact [[Praetor'Varanous|Praetor]] had sent them to retrieve was almost certainly within its walls. [[Berberis]] proposed the approach that would shape their planning: to walk in not as infiltrators, but as a [[Silmara Family|Silmara]] embassy.

#### [[Session 51 - The Last Light]]

In the archives of the Circle of the Scroll, an old archivist named Aaroun reluctantly drew back the curtain on the oldest history of the Vale. He described [[Ryordan Silmara|Ryordan, first of his name]], who sundered the World Tree to save all creation — and how, as the tree withdrew, the sky cracked and a piece of it fell upon the northern Vale, drowning it in endless night. Caer Nystral, the archivist said, was built by the wronged in the crater where the sky had fallen — a deliberate mirror to the Shining City, and a standing grievance older than memory.

#### [[Session 52 - The Star-Veiled Queen]]

The party reached the Dawnless City at last, and were taken by the Hunt before they ever saw its walls — escorted not to the Antler Gates but off the causeway into the Barrow-Ring, where the centaur warden [[Caradoc]], a loyalist of the crone [[Ymrageth]], brought them before her in a barrow-tomb. Bargained into carrying a hidden weapon for her, they were then marched in earnest through the city: in through the Antler Gates, up the Midnight Procession through the lightless Lower Warren, past the worg-kennels and nightmare-stables of the Muster Yards, and through the cold halls of the Court Tiers to the black obsidian of the Veiled Citadel at the summit. There, in a throne room lit by the dark moon through tall stained glass, they were received by [[Mirileth|the Star-Veiled Queen]] herself — their passage confirming, ring by ring, everything the archives of [[Sumara, The Shining City|Sumara]] had recorded of the Dawnless City's shape.

## Trivia

- Where Sumara is named the Shining City, Caer Nystral is named the Dawnless. The two cities are deliberate mirrors of one another, and the people of the Vale never let the comparison be forgotten.
- Its spires are shaped like antlers — sharp, asymmetric, and fae-wrong, with nothing quite square or true.
- No sun has ever touched Caer Nystral. Over uncounted generations its people have come to share its dark; an intruder who cannot see in blackness is blind, and lit to fae eyes like a struck match.
